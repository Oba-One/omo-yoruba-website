How it began, 1997

In 1997 there were maybe four hundred Yoruba families spread across Los Angeles County, and no room any of them could walk into and hear the language spoken back. Naming ceremonies happened in living rooms. Funerals were arranged by whoever could be reached by phone.

Chief Adekunle Balogun and Dr Aduke Sofolahan called a meeting in a church hall on Crenshaw. Thirty-one people came. They agreed on three things that afternoon: the children would learn the language, the community would meet in public at least once a year, and nobody would be buried alone.

The first Saturday class ran that fall with nine children and a borrowed blackboard. The first public gathering, which became Odunde, took six more years to reach Leimert Park.

1997 to now

The years before Odunde, and everything since.

  • 1997

    Omo Yorùbá of Southern California is founded in Los Angeles as a 501(c)(3).

  • 1998 to 2002

    An annual summer camp at Citrus College: the first yearly gathering, and the habit of meeting once a year that Odunde grew out of.

  • 2003

    The first Odunde Festival at Leimert Park Plaza, replacing the camp with a public celebration of the Yoruba new year. Two zones, one drum group, about three hundred people.

  • 2009

    Yoruba language lessons begin, with three levels and a written curriculum.

  • 2014

    Àgbàlá Ọmọde opens as its own zone at the festival, and the children's programs run year-round.

  • 2024

    The Yoruba Cultural Collective forms, and the Solar Hub lights its first community roof.

  • Today

    Online language lessons, two annual events, and programs across Southern California.
