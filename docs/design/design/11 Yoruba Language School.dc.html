Ohun tí a ń kọ́•What we teach

What you learn

Tone marks and underdots are taught from the first lesson. Ẹ, ọ, ṣ, and the three tones are the difference between one word and another, so we never skip them.

Three levels. Learners move up when the teacher says they are ready, not on a calendar.

Level one, first words

Greetings, family, numbers, and the alphabet with its marks. By the end you can greet an elder correctly and say who your people are.

Level two, reading and tone

Reading aloud with the right tone, writing with marks, and everyday sentences. By the end you can read a short passage and be understood.

Level three, conversation

Proverbs, storytelling, and holding a conversation for ten minutes without switching to English. By the end you can call home and stay in Yoruba.

Ẹ̀kọ́ kan•One lesson

What a lesson looks like

About an hour on a call. So a first-time parent knows what to expect.

Before
Join the call

The link is the same every time. Everyone is greeted in Yoruba as they arrive, learners and parents alike.

First half
Lesson by level

New words, tone practice, and reading aloud. Parents are welcome to sit beside a younger child.

Break
Ten minutes off camera

Water, a stretch, and a chance for parents to ask the teacher a quick question.

Second half
Speaking

Conversation with the teacher, and song for younger children. Nobody leaves without speaking.

After
Homework by email

A page of practice, about fifteen minutes' worth, sent the same day.
