Interaction inventory

Every flow across the nine pages

Fourteen interactions. Four patterns. The rule I applied, then each flow with its trigger, fields, success, and failure.

The rule

How I chose between page, inline, modal, and handoff

  • Inline on the page when the form is long, carries an upload or a fee, or needs the surrounding page as context. Losing a modal loses the work.
  • Modal only when a form is short, identical everywhere, and triggered from many pages. Exactly two qualify: the sponsor enquiry and the performer enquiry. Plus the lightbox, which holds no data at all.
  • Its own page when the thing has a URL people share or return to: an album, a filtered news view.
  • Handoff out only where money changes hands. Our page collects the decision, the platform collects the card.

Inline, in placeFooter, every page
Trigger

Already present in the footer. No button opens it.

They fill in

Email. One field, nothing else.

Success

Button becomes "Ẹ ṣé! ✓" in place. No page change, no redirect.

Error

Message under the field: "That email address does not look right. Check it and try again." Their text stays.

Why not a modal: a pop-up asking for an email is the single most disliked pattern on nonprofit sites, and it lands on elders and first-time visitors hardest. It stays in the footer.

Membership join

Inline block/get-involved block 3
Trigger

Become a member, from the nav, the door cards, the homepage, and three take-part bands. All scroll to the same block.

They fill in

Name, email, phone, city, individual or family, hometown association, how they heard, why they are joining. Eight fields, three required.

Success

"Ẹ ṣé! ✓ Welcome." Then a plain paragraph: dues are not paid here, someone will contact them within [pending] days about paying, and here is who.

Error

Field-level messages, entries kept, one summary line at the top naming what is missing. Failure to send shows the email address to write to instead.

The risk: without an explicit next-step sentence, people believe they have joined and paid. That sentence is content I need from you.

Class enrolment

Inline, two steps/programs/language-school block 8
Trigger

Enrol a learner, from the page header, the programs hub, the homepage, and news posts about term dates.

They fill in

Step one, the learner: name, age, level or not sure, term. Step two, the guardian: name, email, phone, notes. An add-another-learner control repeats step one for siblings.

Success

"Ẹ ṣé! ✓ [name] is enrolled for [term]." Then what to bring, where to arrive, and how fees are settled.

Error

Step-level validation before advancing. Nothing is lost moving between steps. If a term is full, a plain message and a waiting-list option rather than a dead end.

Add-another-learner is the whole design. Most families enrol two or three children and will not complete the form three times.

Vendor application

Inline, three steps/odunde block 8
Trigger

Apply for a booth, from the festival header, the take-part band, and a signpost on Get Involved. Get Involved links here rather than duplicating the form.

They fill in

Step one, business and contact. Step two, what they sell, booth size, power, food permit, photographs of their setup. Step three, fees and terms, accepted by checkbox.

Success

"Ẹ ṣé! ✓ Your application is in." Then the review timeline, when they will hear, and that a place is not held until the fee is paid.

Per-step validation. Uploads show progress and a clear message on files that are too large. A failed submission keeps everything and offers the email address.

Never a modal. Uploads, a fee, and terms to read. People start this on a phone and finish it later, so the block also holds what they typed if they navigate away.

Group three, money

Where we hand off, and exactly where the seam falls

Donation, amount and recurring

Inline choiceThen Zeffy
Trigger

The gold Donate button in the nav on every page, plus take-part rows.

They fill in

On our page: one-time or monthly, then an amount or a custom figure. On Zeffy: name, email, card.

Success

Zeffy confirms and receipts. They return to a short thank-you on our site: "Ẹ ṣé! ✓" and one line on what happens next.

Error

Card failures belong to Zeffy. Our side handles only a missing or invalid custom amount, inline.

The seam: the decision is ours, the card is theirs. Amount and frequency are where donors drop out, so that screen stays on your page in your design. Monthly is a toggle above the amounts, never an upsell after they choose.

Gala tickets, seats

Handoff to Eventbrite/gala block 4
Trigger

Get tickets in the header, or a tier card. Opens Eventbrite in a new tab so your page is still there behind it.

They fill in

Everything on Eventbrite: quantity, names, card.

Success

Eventbrite emails the ticket. Our page shows a short "coming back?" line for anyone who returns to the tab.

Eventbrite's. If the event is not yet published, the tier cards show "on sale [date]" rather than a broken link.

Our tier cards, their checkout. An embedded Eventbrite widget is the worst of both: it neither matches your design nor behaves like a native form. A styled card leading out is cleaner and reads as deliberate.

Photo gallery browsing

Album pageLightbox
Trigger

An album tile opens a page. A photograph inside opens the full-screen overlay.

They do

Arrow keys, swipe, click the background or press Escape to close. Caption stays visible.

Success

Not applicable. Closing returns them to the same scroll position, on the photograph they opened.

Applies to all fourteen

Shared conventions

  • Success in Yoruba, next steps in English. "Ẹ ṣé! ✓" opens every confirmation on a form we own, followed by a plain sentence saying what happens next. Money handoffs get the mark on return, not mid-flow.
  • Errors are sentences, not codes. Named field, said plainly, entries never cleared. Colour never carries the message alone.
  • Every form has a human fallback. An email address beside it, so a broken form never ends the relationship.
  • Nothing opens on load. No entry pop-up, no scroll-triggered newsletter, no exit intent.
  • Modals return focus to the button that opened them, close on Escape, and trap focus while open.
  • 44px targets, 17px body, AA contrast in every state, including error text and disabled buttons.

Count

Fourteen interactions. Six inline forms we own, three modals, three handoffs, two browsing behaviours.

The wireframes call for around two dozen new components in total. Six of them carry all fourteen interactions: the enquiry modal, the multi-step inline form, the filter chip row, the amount selector, the tier card, and the lightbox. Build those six first.

Where we can only go so far

You asked for third-party forms in your styling. Where we own the form and submit behind the scenes, that is exact. Where money moves, Eventbrite and Zeffy own the card screen and accept limited styling.

So the seam is placed where it costs least: your design covers the decision, theirs covers the payment. Only two flows cross it, tickets and donations.

Three I would argue about

Gala tables as an enquiry rather than a purchase. Cash flow versus conversion.

Vendor application inline rather than a Google Form. More to build, far better completion.

No newsletter pop-up anywhere. Costs you sign-ups. Worth it.
